## Scope

This reason code applies when the cardholder received the goods or services but
they differ materially from the description given at the point of sale, or they
arrived damaged or defective. Receipt is not in dispute here — the cardholder
acknowledges delivery. A claim that nothing arrived belongs under 13.1.

## Cardholder claim

The issuer must hold a statement describing the discrepancy between what was
described and what was received, in terms specific enough to be assessed. The
cardholder must state that they attempted to return the item or to resolve the
matter with the merchant, and the outcome of that attempt.

## Return requirement

Where the goods are physical and the cardholder still holds them, the dispute
normally requires that the goods were returned or made available for return. A
cardholder who keeps the goods and also receives a refund has been unjustly
enriched, and the merchant may represent on that basis alone.

## Material difference

A material difference is one that affects the value or usability of what was
bought: wrong model, wrong quantity, counterfeit, or a specification that does
not match the listing. Buyer's remorse is not a material difference. Minor
cosmetic variation, or a colour rendering difference between a screen and a
physical item, is generally not material.

## Merchant representment evidence

The merchant defends this dispute by showing that what was delivered matched the
description. Useful evidence includes the original listing or specification
provided to the cardholder, photographs taken before dispatch, a signed
acceptance record, a repair or replacement already provided, and any policy on
returns that was disclosed before purchase and accepted by the cardholder.

## Interaction with refunds already issued

If the merchant already issued a credit for the disputed amount before the
dispute was raised, the dispute is invalid and the merchant represents with the
credit reference. A credit issued after the dispute was raised does not
automatically resolve it and may result in the cardholder being credited twice.

## Time limits

The issuer must raise the dispute within 120 calendar days of the transaction
processing date, or of the date the cardholder received the goods where that is
later, up to 540 days from the transaction. The merchant has 30 calendar days to
represent.

## Common outcomes

Representment succeeds when the merchant can show the listing and a matching
dispatch record, or an accepted return policy the cardholder did not follow. It
fails when the merchant argues only that its policy forbids returns, because a
disclosed no-return policy does not override a material misdescription.
