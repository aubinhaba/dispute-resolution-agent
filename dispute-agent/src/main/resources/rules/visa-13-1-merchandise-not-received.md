## Scope

This reason code applies when the cardholder agreed to the purchase but did not
receive the goods or services by the agreed date, or by 30 calendar days after
the transaction date when no date was agreed. The cardholder recognises the
merchant. This is the key difference from a fraud dispute: identity is not in
question here, delivery is.

## Cardholder claim

The issuer must hold a statement describing what was ordered, what was expected
and what actually arrived. The cardholder must also state that they attempted to
resolve the matter with the merchant, or explain why such an attempt was not
possible. A dispute raised without any contact attempt may be returned to the
issuer as premature.

## Delivery evidence that defends the dispute

The merchant defends this dispute with proof of delivery, not proof of dispatch.
Proof of delivery means a carrier record showing the item was delivered to the
cardholder's address, with a date and a tracking reference that can be verified
independently. A shipping label, an internal warehouse record or an order status
of "shipped" is proof of dispatch only and does not defend the dispute.

## Digital goods and services

For digital goods, delivery is evidenced by download or access logs tied to the
cardholder's account, showing the date and the address from which the content
was retrieved. For services, evidence is a record of the service having been
performed or made available on the agreed date. Continued use of the service
after the disputed date is strong evidence against the cardholder claim.

## Partial and delayed delivery

Where part of an order was delivered, the dispute is limited to the value of the
undelivered part. Where delivery was late but did occur before the dispute was
raised, the merchant may represent with the delivery record; lateness alone does
not entitle the cardholder to a refund unless a delivery date was a stated
condition of the sale.

## Time limits

The issuer must raise the dispute within 120 calendar days of the transaction
processing date, or of the agreed delivery date where that date is later, up to
a maximum of 540 days from the transaction. The merchant has 30 calendar days to
submit representment evidence.

## Common outcomes

Representment succeeds when a carrier tracking record shows delivery to the
cardholder address. It fails when the merchant supplies only its own internal
fulfilment status, because that record is under the merchant's sole control and
is not independently verifiable.
